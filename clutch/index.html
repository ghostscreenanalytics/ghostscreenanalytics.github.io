Clutch: Who Delivers When It's Close

Clutch isn't just the last shot. The NBA defines it as the last five minutes of the fourth quarter or overtime with the score within five points. This model charts every clutch shot by that definition since 1996-97, regular season and playoffs, and asks two questions: who scored the most above average when it was close, and does clutch shooting actually repeat? A Ghost Screen Analytics measure, not an official NBA stat.

Backtest: is clutch real?

If some players have a true clutch gear, their past clutch shooting should predict their future clutch shooting better than anything else. We tested that out of sample. The shrink and blend weights were fit on seasons through 2012-13 only, then every method predicted each player's next clutch season from 2013-14 to 2024-25, using only what was known before that season. Lower error is better; the bar to clear is simply guessing league average for everyone.

What this means

    How it works

    ClutchFourth quarter or overtime, 5:00 or less left, score within five before the play. This is the NBA.com definition, rebuilt play by play, and it matches NBA.com's totals (De'Aaron Fox's 194 clutch points in 2022-23, LeBron James's 197 in 2017-18).
    Points addedClutch points minus league-average clutch points per true shot attempt (FGA + 0.44 × FTA) times his attempts. Regular season and playoffs each use their own season average.
    With turnoversThe same, minus each clutch turnover valued at a league-average clutch possession. Ball handlers drop here, because assists are not credited.
    RiseHow much better (or worse) he shot in the clutch than in the rest of his games, each compared with its own league average, in points per 100 shots.
    Model skillThe backtested estimate of his true clutch shooting: his clutch record shrunk toward average, blended with his non-clutch shooting. Points per 100 clutch shots above average.
    DataNBA.com play-by-play from the open shufinskiy/nba_data repository, 1996-97 through 2024-25. Code: pipeline/clutch_build.py, clutch_model.py, clutch_publish.py.

    What it doesn't know

    • Coverage starts in 1996-97, so Michael Jordan's Bulls years before that, Larry Bird and Magic Johnson aren't in it. Jordan's last two Bulls seasons and his Wizards years are.
    • It measures scoring. Defense, rebounds and passing in the clutch aren't valued, and assists aren't credited in either version.
    • Shot difficulty isn't adjusted. A star taking a contested last-second jumper is compared with the league average on all clutch shots.
    • Late-game fouling sends more free throws to some players than others, which helps good free-throw shooters.
    • These tests were run once, after the data was built. Unlike most Ghost Screen models, the bars were not written down beforehand.
