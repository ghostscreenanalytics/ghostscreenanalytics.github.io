Who's the Most Clutch Player in the NBA Right Now?

The Clutch Score ranks every current NBA player on everything he does in the clutch that the play-by-play can see: how much he scores and how efficiently (turnovers count against him), his passing and offensive rebounds, his defense (steals, blocks, defensive rebounds, charges drawn, and how the other team scores while he's on the floor), and how he shows up in the playoffs, where every clutch play counts double. Clutch uses the NBA's definition: the last five minutes of the fourth quarter or overtime with the score within five. Rankings use the last three seasons, 2023-24 to 2025-26. A Ghost Screen Analytics measure, not an official NBA stat.

The Clutch Score

Each score adds up four parts, all in points. Scoring: clutch volume and efficiency. Playmaking: assists and offensive rebounds. Defense: steals, blocks, defensive rebounds, charges drawn and his share of the team's clutch defense. Big games: all of the above in the playoffs, doubled. Click a player for the full breakdown.

How the score works

Clutch chancesEvery clutch shot (field goal attempts plus 0.44 × free throw attempts) and every clutch turnover.
VolumeChances × the gap between a league-average and a replacement-level clutch scorer ( points per chance; replacement is the bottom 20% of clutch seasons). Taking a lot of clutch shots earns credit, as long as you're not replacement level.
EfficiencyChances × how many more points per chance he scores than the league. His clutch efficiency is pulled toward average on small samples (shrunk by chances / (chances + 300)) and blended 50/50 with his efficiency the rest of the game, because the backtest below shows normal efficiency predicts clutch efficiency better than clutch history alone.
Playmaking0.7 points per clutch assist and per offensive rebound (John Hollinger's Game Score weights).
Defense plays1.0 per steal, 0.7 per block, 0.3 per defensive rebound (Game Score weights) and 1.0 per charge drawn, which takes away a possession like a steal. Fouls aren't penalized, because late-game fouls are often on purpose; their cost already shows up in the next piece.
Team defense on the floorHow many fewer points per chance the other team scored while he was on the floor, compared with the league, times those chances, split five ways. Pulled hard toward zero on small samples (chances / (chances + 1,000)), because on-court numbers are noisy and shared with teammates. Lineups are rebuilt from substitutions for every fourth quarter and overtime.
Big gamesEverything above, from playoff clutch minutes, counted twice (scoring is measured against a replacement-level closer). Players whose teams missed the playoffs get zero here, not a penalty.
WindowRegular season and playoffs, 2023-24 through 2025-26. Career view: every season since 1996-97.

Backtest: does adding defense help?

The real goal in the clutch is outscoring the other team. So the test asks: using only a player's previous three seasons, which version best ranks how his team does in clutch minutes with him on the floor the next regular season (team points minus opponent points, compared with the league)? Every season from 2000-01 on. Weights were set before the test; seasons through 2012-13 are shown separately from 2013-14 to 2025-26.

What it doesn't know

  • Defense without tracking data is partial. One-on-one defense, contests and switches don't show up in play-by-play, so a great on-ball defender who doesn't get steals or blocks is undersold.
  • Team defense on the floor is shared with four teammates and the coach's scheme, which is why it's split five ways and shrunk.
  • Lineups couldn't be rebuilt for about 3% of fourth quarters and overtimes; those count for individual stats but not team defense.
  • Shot difficulty isn't adjusted. A contested pull-up in the final seconds counts the same as an open layup.
  • Late-game fouling sends more free throws to some players, which helps good free-throw shooters.
  • Playoff value depends on making the playoffs. A great closer on a lottery team can't earn big-game credit.
  • The 2026-27 season isn't included yet. Rosters are current; the clutch numbers run through the 2026 playoffs.

Data: NBA.com play-by-play via the open shufinskiy/nba_data repository, 1996-97 through the 2026 playoffs. Season totals match NBA.com's clutch stats (Shai Gilgeous-Alexander's 175 clutch points in 2025-26, De'Aaron Fox's 194 in 2022-23). Code: pipeline/clutch_*.py.
